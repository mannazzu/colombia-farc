Act I & II: Historical Roots, Agrarian Resistance & Rise of FARC-EP 1948–1982

Pillar 1

⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: Gaitán Murder 1948 ──► El Bogotazo ──► Chulavita Terror ──► Massive Land Dispossession

1. La Violencia & Economic Dispossession

1. The 1948 Spark: Populist leader Jorge Eliécer Gaitán was assassinated, triggering El Bogotazo and ten years of bloody civil war known as La Violencia with over two hundred thousand deaths. Elite monopoly left over 80% of fertile land in the hands of one percent of landowners.

2. Chulavita Paramilitarism: Government-armed Conservative militias known as Chulavitas attacked Liberal peasants with total impunity under local landlord control known as Gamonalismo.

3. Economic Tool of Terror: Violence forced peasants off fertile coffee lands in an economic dispossession tactic called El Despojo, allowing big landowners to seize working plantations at zero cost.

📷 Jorge Eliécer Gaitán & Bipartisan Violence 1948–1953
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: 1953 Amnesty Trap ──► Peasant Enclaves ──► Marquetalia Attack 1964 ──► FARC Founding 1966

2. Independent Republics & Birth of FARC

1. The Amnesty Trap of 1953: Peasants who surrendered arms were murdered by landlord militias. Manuel Marulanda Tirofijo refused to disarm and retreated into the jungle.

2. Independent Republics: Displaced families built autonomous farming zones in areas such as Marquetalia, which politicians falsely labeled as an illegal communist threat.

3. Operation Marquetalia of 1964: Sixteen thousand state troops backed by the United States attacked 48 armed peasants. This forced a shift from self-defense to a mobile national army, founding FARC in 1966.

📷 Manuel Marulanda Tirofijo & Jacobo Arenas — FARC Founders
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: PCC Frontier Governance ──► Coca Gramaje ──► Rise of MAS ──► 7th Conference Army of the People

3. Coca Boom & Transformation into FARC Army of the People

1. De Facto Statehood from 1966 to 1977: Guided by the Colombian Communist Party PCC, FARC enforced local order and settled land disputes in remote jungle frontiers such as Caquetá and Meta.

2. The Coca Boom from 1978 to 1981: Taxing the coca paste trade through a system called gramaje provided financial independence. This expansion triggered violent backlash from drug lords, creating MAS in 1981 as the first modern paramilitary group.

3. Birth of FARC Army of the People in 1982: At their Seventh Conference, FARC adopted the Army of the People suffix, creating an 80-front plan to encircle cities and force state negotiations in 1984.

Act III: La Uribe Accords & Genocide of Unión Patriótica 1984–1990s

Pillar 1
1984–1985 | The La Uribe Accords & UP Test

1987–1990s | Plan Baile Rojo & End of Peace
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: Betancur Peace Plan ──► 1984 Ceasefire ──► Peace Without Disarming ──► Creation of UP 1985

1. The 1984 Accords & Democratic Test

1. The 1984 Accords: President Belisario Betancur accepted that war was caused by poverty and political exclusion, signing a bilateral ceasefire with FARC.

2. Testing Democracy: Rather than surrendering their weapons first, FARC created Unión Patriótica in 1985 as a legal party to test if the state would allow peaceful left-wing opposition.

3. Broad Coalition: Unión Patriótica united demobilized guerrillas, Communist Party members, trade unions, peasant leaders, and left-wing intellectuals.

📷 Mass Mobilization & Civic Support for Democratic Opening 1985
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: UP Platform ──► 1986 Election Victory ──► Threat to Elite Power ──► Landlord & Cartel Backlash

2. Electoral Success & Elite Backlash

1. Big Election Wins in 1986: Unión Patriótica broke the historic Liberal-Conservative monopoly, winning 14 seats in Congress, 18 mayoralties, and over 350 local council seats.

2. Presidential Vote: Candidate Jaime Pardo Leal won over four percent of the national vote with three hundred twenty-eight thousand votes, setting a historic record for the Colombian left.

3. Elite Backlash: Rapid growth of Unión Patriótica in rural areas such as Urabá, Meta, and Caquetá frightened rich landowners, military commanders, and drug cartels such as the Medellín Cartel.

📷 Jaime Pardo Leal — Presidential Candidate & Historic Electoral Breakthrough 1986
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: Plan Baile Rojo ──► Murder of Candidates ──► Political Genocide ──► Return to 30 Years of War

3. Plan Baile Rojo & Political Genocide

1. Plan Baile Rojo: State intelligence, rich landowners, and drug paramilitaries MAS launched a systematic plan to murder members of Unión Patriótica.

2. Decapitation of Leaders: Murdered two presidential candidates, Jaime Pardo Leal in 1987 and Bernardo Jaramillo Ossa in 1990, alongside twenty-four congressmen and mayors and thousands of activists.

Act IV: Escalation, Military Stalemate & Path to Peace 1990–2016

Pillar 1
1990–2002 | War of Movement & US Plan Colombia
Pillar 2
2002–2010 | "Seguridad Democrática" & Leadership Decapitation
Pillar 3
2010–2016 | Military Stalemate & Transition to Havana
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: Closed Legal Politics ──► War of Movement ──► AUC Terror ──► US Plan Colombia 2000

1. War of Movement & US Plan Colombia

1. Open Warfare & War Economy: Following the UP genocide, FARC shifted to large army operations, raiding military bases and funding 20,000 fighters through coca taxation and highway kidnappings known as pescas milagrosas.

2. AUC Paramilitary Terror: In 1997, landowners, cartels, and military sectors created the AUC paramilitaries, launching systematic massacres against civilians in places such as Mapiripán and El Salado.

3. Caguán Collapse & Plan Colombia: Peace talks in the demilitarized Caguán zone failed in an episode remembered as La Silla Vacía. In response, Colombia and the United States launched Plan Colombia in 2000 with over 7 billion dollars in military aid.

📷 La Silla Vacía 1999
📷 Plan Colombia 2000 Airstrikes
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: Uribe Offensives ──► Jungle Retreat ──► Decapitation of Command ──► Falsos Positivos Crisis

2. "Seguridad Democrática" & Decapitation

1. Full-Scale State Offensive: President Álvaro Uribe launched Seguridad Democrática under Plan Patriota, clearing highways and pushing FARC columns into remote border jungles near Ecuador and Venezuela.

2. Decapitation of High Command: Precision strikes backed by the United States killed top leaders Raúl Reyes in 2008, Mono Jojoy in 2010, and Alfonso Cano in 2011, while founder Manuel Marulanda died naturally in 2008.

3. Falsos Positivos Crisis: Intense pressure for body counts led army units to execute over 6,400 innocent civilians, falsely presenting them as guerrillas killed in combat.

📷 Falsos Positivos Crisis — 6,400+ Executed Civilians
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: Military Deadlock ──► Santos Presidency 2010 ──► Secret Exploration ──► 2016 Havana Accords

3. Military Stalemate & Transition to Havana

1. Recognition of Stalemate: Elected in 2010, President Juan Manuel Santos recognized that while FARC was weakened, the military could not defeat them completely without endless war.

2. Secret Peace Talks: Santos and new FARC leader Timoleón Jiménez Timochenko initiated secret exploratory talks to build a strict six-point peace agenda.

📌 KEY TAKEAWAY: Both sides agreed that political negotiation was the only way out, laying the ground for the historic 2016 Havana Peace Agreement.

⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: Territorial Warfare ──► 8.5M+ Internal Displacement ──► 100k+ Disappeared ──► 11k+ Landmine Victims

1. Dimensions of Human Trauma

1. Forced Displacement: Armed groups forced over 8.5 million people off their lands, making Colombia one of the countries with the highest internal displacement globally.

2. Enforced Disappearances: Over 100,000 people were forcibly disappeared by paramilitaries, state forces, and guerrillas combined.

3. Landmine Legacy: Improvised anti-personnel landmines left Colombia as the second most mine-affected country in the world, causing 11,000+ casualties among soldiers and civilians.

📷 Dimensions of Trauma — Over 8.5 Million Internally Displaced Persons

2. Truth Commission & Responsibility Breakdown

1. Massive Civilian Toll: Over 60 years of war caused between 260,000 and 450,000 deaths, with over 80% being non-combatant civilians.

2. Paramilitary Responsibility Share: AUC paramilitaries were responsible for 45% of total homicide victims during the conflict.

3. Guerrilla and State Responsibility Share: FARC guerrillas were responsible for 21% of total homicides, while state security forces were responsible for 12% of direct homicides alongside documented complicity in paramilitary actions.

Act VI: The 2016 Havana Peace Accord — Breakdown of the 6 Core Points

Pillar 1
Points 1 & 2 | Structural Roots & Democratic Opening
Pillar 2
Points 3 & 4 | End of Conflict & Illegal Economies
Pillar 3
Points 5 & 6 | Transitional Justice & UN Verification
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: Structural Land Inequality ──► Rural Investment RRI ──► Political Access ──► CITREP Victims Seats

1. Structural Roots & Democratic Opening

1. Point 1 — Comprehensive Rural Reform: Fixes historical root causes by creating a 3-million-hectare Land Fund, giving legal titles for 7 million hectares to peasants, and building infrastructure in 16 high-conflict zones known as PDET.

2. Point 2 — Political Participation & CITREP: Opens democratic space by turning FARC into a legal political party named Comunes and reserving 16 Special Peace Seats CITREP in Congress strictly for victims of the conflict.

📷 Points 1 & 2 — Comprehensive Rural Reform RRI & CITREP Peace Seats for Victims
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: Bilateral Ceasefire ──► 7,132 Weapons Handover ──► Crop Substitution PNIS ──► Public Health Model

2. End of Conflict & Illegal Economies

1. Point 3 — End of Conflict & UN Disarmament: Establishes a bilateral ceasefire and gathers fighters into 26 transition zones, leading to the complete handover of 7,132 weapons verified by the UN.

2. Point 4 — Solution to Illicit Crops: Replaces forced crop destruction with voluntary substitution agreements through PNIS, treats drug use as a public health issue, and focuses police force on cartels and money laundering.

📷 Points 3 & 4 — UN-Monitored Disarmament & PNIS Crop Substitution
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: Restorative Justice System ──► JEP Court Hearings ──► UN Security Council Mandate ──► International Verification

3. Transitional Justice & UN Verification

1. Point 5 — Victims Rights & The JEP Court: Creates a comprehensive system with the Truth Commission, the Search Unit for Disappeared Persons, and the Special Jurisdiction for Peace JEP, prioritizing restorative truth and reparations over traditional prison sentences.

2. Point 6 — Implementation & UN Verification: Sets up a follow-up committee CSIVI backed by UN Security Council Resolutions, deploying international UN observers to guarantee long-term compliance.

Module 2 • Restorative Justice, Victim Centrality & Real Cases

Act VII: Transitional Justice & The Special Jurisdiction for Peace

Pillar 1
The Transitional Model | Restorative vs Retributive Justice
Pillar 2
The JEP Court Mechanics | Truth for Conditional Sanctions
Pillar 3
Victim Centrality | Public Recognition Hearings
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: Standard Retributive Jail ──► Restorative Truth Model ──► Community Work ──► Sustainable Peace

1. The Transitional Model — Restorative vs Retributive

1. Beyond Standard Punishment: Traditional courts focus only on prison time, but transitional justice balances punishment with victim rights to prevent endless war and build lasting peace.

2. Restorative Sanctions: Demobilized fighters who tell the full truth do not go to ordinary prisons, but serve five to eight years performing community restorative works under UN supervision.

3. Real Example of Humanitarian Landmine Clearing: Former FARC combatants worked directly alongside military units in rural locations such as El Orejón to remove anti-personnel landmines and restore farmland to local peasants.

📷 Restorative Sanctions — Humanitarian Demining Operations by Demobilized Combatants
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: Immediate Full Truth ──► Restricted Freedom vs Lying ──► 20-Year Prison Term

2. The JEP Court Mechanics & Conditional Sanctions

1. Three Tiers of Sanctions: Full immediate truth yields five to eight years of restricted freedom, delayed truth leads to standard prison, and denying guilt leads to twenty years in prison.

2. Broad Jurisdiction: The court judges demobilized guerrillas, state military personnel, and civilian elites who funded illegal armed groups.

3. Real Example of Institutional Participation: Victims participate actively across macro-cases, confronting former leaders like Timochenko for kidnappings and military officers for extrajudicial executions.

📷 Institutional Framework — Active Victim Participation in the JEP Process
⚡ CAUSAL CHAIN: Direct Victim Confrontation ──► Public Confession ──► Restorative Truth ──► Strict Conditional Protection

3. Victim Centrality & Public Recognition Hearings

1. Active Victim Participation: Victims are not passive observers, but directly question perpetrators in open court to demand answers about their missing relatives.

2. Strict Conditional Protection: Legal benefits are revocable, meaning any perpetrator who hides truth or returns to crime loses all protection and is transferred to standard criminal courts.

3. Real Example of Public Recognition Hearings: At the historic Ocaña hearings, army officers publicly confessed face to face with grieving mothers, revealing the exact locations where executed civilians were buried.
